# What editing against AI patterns gets wrong

Editing against AI patterns has its own signature. A model told to be punchy
or plain strips connectives, breaks sentences into fragments, and flattens
the register until nobody is speaking. Vendors now counter-steer it:
Anthropic tells Fable 5 to keep output short by selection, "not to compress
the writing into fragments, abbreviations, arrow chains ... or jargon", and
OpenAI's GPT-5.4 system prompt says "Do not use incomplete sentences or
abbreviations to avoid dense, cramped writing". Both agree: if you have to
choose between short and clear, choose clear.

The test for every cut: did the reader lose information or a connection? If
yes, the cut was wrong, however much cleaner the result looks.

## Compression into fragments and slogans

"Move faster. Your competition is." "Not a detail. A design decision." "No
fluff. Just results." "Boring wins. Every time." These are the staccato
stack, a documented tell of its own. The reader gets rhythm instead of
reasoning.

Write complete sentences. Cut whole clauses that carry nothing, and keep the
words that carry the logic.

## Stripped connectives

Because, so, but, unless, which means, even though. Cutting them makes each
sentence stand alone and leaves the reader to rebuild the argument. Keep the
connective when the next sentence depends on the previous one. Cut it when
the sentences are independent.

## Blanket adverb bans

Model text already has fewer hedges, boosters, and stance markers than
human text, and that impersonal, expository register is itself a measured
tell. Removing every adverb pushes prose further toward it. An adverb that
changes the meaning stays: only, still, already, not yet, rarely. The
sincerity ones go: genuinely, honestly, truly, really.

## Blanket punctuation bans

Models use fewer commas, semicolons, and parentheses than people do. A
reasonable aside in parentheses is a human habit, not a machine one. The em
dash is now a weak signal outside Claude; the problem was the dash as a
universal connector. Pete's own register uses no em dashes and no
parentheticals, so apply those rules for text he will read. For other
readers, judge the mark by whether it helps the sentence.

## Two beats three

Sometimes there are three. Use the real count. The rule of three is a tell
when the third item is padding or the count comes from rhythm.

## Passive voice and Wh- openers

No vendor guide or corpus study names either as a machine marker. Passive
voice is right when the actor is unknown or beside the point: "the
certificate was revoked in March". "What changed is the default timeout" is
a fine sentence when the change is the topic. Fix passives that hide who
decided. Leave the rest.

## Uniformity in the other direction

Every paragraph two sentences. Every sentence ten words. Every ending flat.
Human writing varies more than this, and measured sentence-length variance
is one of the clearest current markers of machine text. Let a sentence run
long when the thought does. Let a paragraph be one line when that is all
there is.

## Hedge removal that upgrades a guess

"I think", "in my experience", "this may", "seems" belong on the claim that
is uncertain. Removing them to sound confident produces a hedged-wrong or
confident-wrong answer, and both are worse than a hedged-right one. Keep the
qualification attached to the claim it qualifies and remove it from claims
that are established.

## Neutralized voice

Removing warmth, judgment, humor, exclamation, and asides produces the flat
expository register that readers already associate with machines. Preserve
how strongly the writer meant the reaction. Delight and irritation can be
stated plainly; they do not need to become a balanced assessment.

## Anti-formatting by rule

The 2024 and 2025 fight was against bullets and bold. Fable 5.1 formats
less, and Anthropic now says anti-formatting rules "suppress structure the
content needs". A list of parallel items is a list. Steps are numbered. A
comparison is a table. Cramming those into a paragraph is not plainness.

## Word-list hunting

Lists of three hundred banned words include "however", "additionally", and
"ensure". Stripping ordinary connectives and verbs leaves gaps, not clarity.
Replace a word only when the ordinary word says the same thing better, and
never replace one tell with another: "load-bearing" to "critical"
everywhere, "it's not X, it's Y" to "X, not Y", "delve" to "explore". The
fix is the literal statement of what is there.

## Scoring instead of editing

A self-assigned score on directness or authenticity does not change a
sentence. The checks in the skill do. Spend the time on the passes, not on
the number.
